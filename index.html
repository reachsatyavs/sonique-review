What we found

23 of 31 distortion readings are over 2.5 %, and 13 of 64 level readings are outside tolerance. Almost all of the distortion is the 3rd harmonic on the headphones and the 2nd harmonic on the bone vibrator at 250 Hz.

  • ✕High impact
    Headphone distortion between 90 and 115 dB HL. 500 Hz, 1 kHz and 2 kHz all exceed 2.5 % THD in this range, peaking at 7.14 % (2 kHz, 100 dB HL) and 6.73 % (1 kHz, 105 dB HL). The 3rd harmonic accounts for nearly all of it, and THD falls again above 110 dB HL. The audiologist suggests filtering at the 3rd harmonic.
  • ✕High impact
    Bone vibrator overdriven at 250 Hz. THD climbs from 3.8 % at 40 dB HL to 25.7 % at 60 dB HL (2nd harmonic), and the level runs 1–4 dB high. Only levels up to 35 dB HL are clean.
  • ✕High impact
    Warble tone is far off. Carrier 1052 Hz instead of 1000, deviation 85.3 % (should be under 15 %), modulation rate 39.8 Hz (Interacoustics: 5 Hz).
  • ✕High impact
    Pulse timing uneven. 133.9 ms on and 265.7 ms off. The audiologist's target is 500 ms on and 500 ms off, as the Interacoustics audiometer gives (495.8 / 504.2 ms).
  • ✕High impact
    Noise signals are not to specification. White noise is shaped like speech noise rather than flat from 250 Hz to 20 kHz, and reads 5–9 dB high. Narrow-band noise is broadband, not a narrow peak around the centre frequency.
  • !Near limit
    Level dips at 4 and 8 kHz. 4 kHz runs up to 2 dB low between 95 and 100 dB HL; 8 kHz up to 3.3 dB low between 85 and 95 dB HL. Both recover above 105 dB HL.
  • !Near limit
    Bone vibrator offsets and ceiling. 500 Hz and 2 kHz are 4–4.5 dB high even after −20 dB of calibration; 4 kHz cannot go above 91.4 dB (60 dB HL usable).
  • ✓Pass
    Good areas. Low and mid levels are linear on the headphones; the bone vibrator at 1 kHz is within 0.5 dB from 20 to 70 dB HL; 4 kHz headphones show no distortion.

Action items

Firmware side changes (probable)

  1. Reduce 3rd-harmonic distortion on the headphones at 90–115 dB HL (500 Hz–2 kHz), for example with a filter at the 3rd harmonic, and check the output path for clipping at those levels.
  2. Correct level linearity at 4 kHz (85–105 dB HL) and 8 kHz (75–95 dB HL).
  3. Warble: 1000 Hz carrier, about 5 Hz modulation rate, deviation under 15 %.
  4. Pulse: 500 ms on and 500 ms off, with the rise, plateau and fall in photo 30.
  5. White noise flat from 250 Hz to 20 kHz.
  6. Narrow-band noise as a true narrow band around each centre frequency.
  7. Store calibration values for white noise and narrow-band noise per frequency (the current calibration commands have no room for them).
  8. Calibration resolution of 0.1 dB in place of 1 dB.
  9. Bone vibrator: limit 250 Hz output to the clean range, and find why 4 kHz stops at 91.4 dB.
  10. New graphs on the device for tone decay and speech test.
  11. A device-to-app command whenever the patient releases the response button, so the app can capture it.

App side changes

  1. Fix free-field calibration: changing a free-field value does not change the tone today, unlike headphones and bone.
  2. Raise the white-noise limit to 95 dB.
  3. Calibration screen: 0.1 dB up/down steps (needs firmware item 8).
  4. Calibration screen: rows for white noise and narrow-band noise (needs firmware item 7).
  5. Per-frequency maximum levels from these readings: headphones 250 Hz ≤ 90 dB HL and 8 kHz ≤ 110 dB HL; bone 250 Hz ≤ 35–40 dB HL and 4 kHz ≤ 60 dB HL.
  6. Bone calibration: about −4 dB more at 500 Hz and −5 dB more at 2 kHz (to −25), then re-measure.
  7. Tone decay: whenever the patient releases the button, the app captures it and raises the level by 5 dB (needs firmware item 11).

Specific features for tone decay

  • Example run: 2 kHz at 60 dB. Starting the timer starts the test and a continuous tone.
  • Each time the patient releases the response button, the level goes up 5 dB, for as long as the 60-second test runs.
  • The button release comes from the device into the app.

Specific features for speech test

  • A VU meter on the speech screen.
  • Microphone calibration with −6, −3, 0 and +3 dB settings.
  • The audiologist speaks the words and guides the patient through one microphone, and monitors through a headphone with a monitor mic. The patient uses headphones or free field.
  • The audiologist's microphone is off during pure-tone tests.
  • The audiologist can see when the patient responds to a tone.
